## 2. Methods (as run)

- **Primary metric `Reach_N(L)`** = fraction of (source neuron, sink neuron) pairs connected by a directed path of ≤L hops through surviving neurons, as the preregistration specifies. Paths are *strict*: they may relay only through removable interneurons, so sensory→sensory and DN→DN chains cannot act as an undamageable backbone. Sources are a fixed sample of 128 sensory neurons stratified by modality group (class × side). L = 4, 6 (primary) and "∞" (iterated to convergence, capped at 30 hops in reduced runs; the uncapped first runs used 60).
- **Secondary metrics:** `Reach_G` (modality-group × sink pairs, strict); `Reach_G,loose` (relays through any neuron); **path redundancy** = Σ_sinks log(1 + number of walks of length 1–6 from the whole source set) on the binarised strict graph; **weighted influence** = Σ_sinks of summed products of input-normalised weights over walks ≤6 hops (prereg "weighted variant"); **giant SCC** fraction (scipy csgraph, computed for the first 3 orders of each regime). Sink subsets DN vs MN are also reported in `curves_normalised_all.csv.gz`.
- Engine: pure numpy bitset propagation (64 sources per uint64 word) with frontier-delta updates, plus scipy.sparse; no networkx on whole graphs. One dataset was processed per process with int32 indices.
- **Damage regimes:** (a) random; (b) metabolic-vulnerability-weighted, removal probability ∝ demand (Efraimidis–Spirakis weighted order). Demand is membrane area (`cell_stats.area_nm`, FAFB only, `metabolic_area`) or total synapse count (all datasets, `metabolic_syn`). (c) Hub-targeted static total degree in the ≥5 graph (`degree`), plus a fine low-f grid (`degree_finegrid`, f ≤ 0.3, 19 points). (d) Focal neuropil lesion: grows from a random core neuropil along the neuropil co-innervation (cosine) graph; neurons are removed by the rank of their dominant neuropil. (e) H2 control: degree-bin-matched random (40 log-degree bins).
- f grid: 24 points from 0 to 0.9 for the first orders, then a 13-point subset (all points are on the 24-point grid).
- **Fits:** pooled points from all orders. Models: 4-parameter logistic, linear, and 3-parameter exponential, compared by Gaussian AIC. ΔAIC = AIC_logistic − min(AIC_linear, AIC_exp). f_c = logistic midpoint; w = 10–90% width = 2·ln9·s. H1 categories follow the preregistered thresholds exactly. D50 = first f at which the normalised curve falls to ≤0.5, per order; it is censored when this never happens by f = 0.9.
- **Ratios targeted/random:** bootstrap over removal orders with logistic refits. There were 2000 resamples for the primary metric on the ≥5 graph and 100 for controls and secondary metrics; the CI is the 2.5–97.5 percentile. Note that degree-targeted orders differ only by tie-breaking, so ratio CIs mostly reflect random-arm variability and order sampling, not connectome uncertainty.
- **Controls:** degree-preserving directed rewired null (target shuffling, duplicates and self-loops removed; FAFB and MANC); edge cut-offs ≥10 (all) and ≥1 (MANC). Negative control: remove all removable neurons lying on no strict source→sink path.
- **Keystones:** candidate types = top-150 by flow centrality, i.e. the sum over a type's neurons of (walks ≤3 from sources) × (walks ≤3 to sinks), strict. Then each other dataset's top-50 names present in a given dataset were also evaluated there (replication pass). For each type, all its removable neurons were removed and relative drops in Reach_N(L6, ∞), Reach_G, redundancy and influence were recorded. Ranking: relative ΔReach_N(L6), then relative Δinfluence. Names were matched exactly, case-insensitive.

Reading the table:
- **Random loss is graceful in every dataset.** The mean Reach_N(L6) stays ≥0.8 of baseline up to f = 0.50 (FAFB), 0.45 (BANC), 0.65 (MCNS) and 0.85 (MANC), and falls to 50% only near f ≈ 0.7–0.9 (D50: FAFB 0.759, BANC 0.706, MCNS 0.869, MANC >0.9). The logistic is strongly preferred by AIC, but the transition is wide (w = FAFB 0.671, BANC 0.734, MCNS 0.744, MANC 0.393), above the preregistered falsification bound of 0.25.
- **Metabolic (size/synapse-weighted) loss is far more destructive than random.** In FAFB the membrane-area-weighted f_c is 0.277 and the synapse-weighted f_c is 0.228, versus 0.800 for random. The transition is still graded (w = 0.421 / 0.419).
- **Hub-targeted loss collapses reach almost immediately in brain-containing datasets.** Fine-grid f_c is FAFB 0.033, BANC 0.017, MCNS 0.058, but MANC 0.388 (coarse grid, in range; the fine-grid midpoint 0.421 is extrapolated beyond the fine grid's max f = 0.3). The curves fall from the first removals onward, with no plateau. Exponential is the best non-logistic model for FAFB/BANC/MCNS (Fig. 1), so the "stays near baseline, then collapses" shape that H1 posits is absent even here. Only BANC meets the sharpness criteria (w = 0.086, ΔAIC = -84.7).
- **Degree-bin-matched random removal reproduces the hub-targeted curve almost exactly** (FAFB ratio 0.044 [0.044, 0.045] vs hub-targeted 0.044 [0.044, 0.044]). Vulnerability is carried by degree itself, not by the identity of particular hub neurons.
- **Focal neuropil lesions are core-dependent.** Lesions grown from GNG or VNC neuropil cores sever reach early (per-core D50: BANC GNG 0.109, IntTct 0.109, VNC_T3_MetaNm_L 0.089; MCNS ANm 0.123, LegNp_T1_L 0.119; FAFB GNG 0.317). Optic-lobe and mushroom-body cores barely matter (FAFB LOP_R 0.784, ME_L 0.768, MB_CA_R 0.806; BANC LO_R 0.824; MCNS LO_R 0.862). This gives very wide bands (Fig. 1) and wide ratio CIs. The logistic is not preferred for focal curves (ΔAIC > −2).

### 3.3 Controls (Fig. 3, `tables/*_curves_controls.csv`)

- **Rewired null (degree-preserving).** FAFB random f_c 0.893 (real 0.800); hub-targeted f_c 0.266 (real 0.035). MANC random f_c 1.107 (extrapolated; real 1.027); hub-targeted 0.613 (real 0.388). The real wiring is **more** fragile to hub loss than a random graph with the same degree sequence: sensorimotor routes are concentrated through hubs beyond what the degree sequence alone implies. The graded shape under random loss is shared with the null, so it is not specific to the real wiring.
- **Edge cut-off.** At ≥10 synapses, FAFB random f_c = 0.607, w = 0.960 (falsified). Hub-targeted f_c = 0.013, w = 0.087, ΔAIC = 0.2 (falsified). In MANC at ≥1 synapse, random f_c = 0.787, w = 0.124 (indeterminate (graded transition)), and hub-targeted f_c = 0.770: in the dense ≥1 graph, hub targeting loses most of its advantage. The H1 category flips between cut-offs for the hub-targeted regime (FAFB) and the random regime (MANC). By prereg rule 5, the H1 outcome for these two regimes is therefore **not robust to the edge cut-off** (H1-R3 partly supported). The FAFB random-loss outcome (falsified) is unchanged at ≥10.
- **Negative control (off-path neurons removed):** Δ = 0 exactly for every metric in all datasets. The fractions of removable neurons lying on no strict sensory→sink path were FAFB 2.1%, BANC 27.0%, MCNS 2.4%, MANC 3.2%. BANC's large off-path fraction reflects its ≥3 floor and many weakly connected neurons once the ≥5 cut-off is applied.
- **Susceptibility signature (variance across orders peaking within ±0.05 of f_c):** BANC degree_finegrid: peak f=0.020 vs f_c=0.017 (yes); BANC metabolic_syn: peak f=0.100 vs f_c=0.107 (yes); BANC random: peak f=0.800 vs f_c=0.750 (no); FAFB degree_finegrid: peak f=0.100 vs f_c=0.033 (no); FAFB metabolic_syn: peak f=0.200 vs f_c=0.228 (yes); FAFB random: peak f=0.650 vs f_c=0.800 (no); MANC degree_finegrid: peak f=0.300 vs f_c=0.421 (extrapolated beyond fine-grid max f = 0.3; not interpretable); MANC metabolic_syn: peak f=0.700 vs f_c=0.677 (yes); MANC random: peak f=0.900 vs f_c=1.027 (no); MCNS degree_finegrid: peak f=0.025 vs f_c=0.058 (yes); MCNS metabolic_syn: peak f=0.400 vs f_c=0.304 (no); MCNS random: peak f=0.900 vs f_c=0.951 (no).

Under **random loss**, in all four datasets, **weighted influence falls to 50% well before binary neuron-pair reachability**. D50 influence vs Reach_N L4 / L6: FAFB 0.497 vs 0.626 / 0.759; BANC 0.295 vs 0.554 / 0.706; MCNS 0.327 vs 0.806 / 0.869; MANC 0.240 vs 0.884 / >0.9. The giant SCC (the recurrent core) also fragments early (D50 0.506 FAFB, 0.417 BANC, 0.528 MCNS, 0.656 MANC). Under **targeted** regimes (metabolic, hub), influence and ≤4-hop reach collapse together and the ordering between them is not consistent. Across all regimes, short-path reach fails before long-path reach (D50 L4 < L6 < ∞). Modality-level reach is among the most robust metrics: a modality keeps *some* route long after most individual neuron pairs have lost theirs. Under random loss this is the structural precondition for the H5 "resuscitated but different" regime: feedforward sensorimotor reach stays high while weighted routing and the recurrent core have already degraded. Whether FII follows is for the simulator (dynamical H5) to test. Under hub-targeted loss the order reverses: sensorimotor reach collapses long before the recurrent core fragments. GSCC D50 on the coarse degree grid is FAFB 0.326, BANC 0.108, MCNS 0.394, MANC 0.533, versus Reach_N L6 D50 FAFB 0.075, BANC 0.030, MCNS 0.115, MANC 0.384. Fine-grid rows (all four datasets, verified from `*_curves_fine.csv`: max f = 0.3) stop at f = 0.3, so their censored values read '>0.3'. The MANC fine-grid f_c (0.421) is a logistic extrapolation beyond that range; the coarse-grid MANC degree f_c (0.388, D50 0.384) is the in-range estimate.

### 3.5 Keystone cell types (Fig. 4, `tables/*_keystone*.csv`, `keystone_replication.csv`, `keystone_overlap.csv`)

- **No single cell type is a structural keystone.** Removing every removable neuron of any one evaluated type removes at most ~1% of strict sensory→sink pairs:
  - FAFB: 154 types evaluated, 20 with any Reach loss, max relative ΔReach_N(L6) = 0.88% (CB1231), max Δinfluence = 2.69% (CB1231)
  - BANC: 218 types evaluated, 143 with any Reach loss, max relative ΔReach_N(L6) = 1.03% (IN13B010), max Δinfluence = 1.19% (CB1231)
  - MCNS: 194 types evaluated, 73 with any Reach loss, max relative ΔReach_N(L6) = 0.86% (AN04A001), max Δinfluence = 0.66% (AN19B001)
  - MANC: 161 types evaluated, 0 with any Reach loss, max relative ΔReach_N(L6) = 0.00% (AN19B001), max Δinfluence = 1.58% (IN10B011)
- Because DNs (including the giant fibre DNp01, DNa/DNp types) are sinks in FAFB, BANC and MCNS, they are protected by design and could not be tested as keystones there. In MANC, where DNs are interneurons, only 3 DN types entered the flow-centrality candidate set and none ranked in the top-50.
- Replication of top-50 *membership* (exact type names) does not exceed chance once the universe is restricted to types evaluated in both datasets: FAFB–BANC: 13 shared of 48/18 (expected 10.4, p = 0.129); FAFB–MCNS: 5 shared of 32/10 (expected 5.0, p = 0.634); BANC–MCNS: 15 shared of 46/48 (expected 14.9, p = 0.560); BANC–MANC: 16 shared of 32/50 (expected 14.7, p = 0.364); MCNS–MANC: 20 shared of 41/48 (expected 17.1, p = 0.173). The naive universe (all typed removable types) gives p = 1e-9 to 1e-27, but that is an artefact of preselection by flow centrality and is not used for inference.
- **Graded importance does replicate.** Across types evaluated in both datasets, the Spearman correlation of relative influence loss is FAFB–BANC ρ = 0.730 (n = 83, p = 5.2e-15); FAFB–MCNS ρ = 0.710 (n = 64, p = 5.1e-11); BANC–MCNS ρ = 0.911 (n = 148, p = 3.9e-58); BANC–MANC ρ = 0.652 (n = 109, p = 1.6e-14); MCNS–MANC ρ = 0.776 (n = 115, p = 2.2e-24) (FAFB–MANC share only 3 type names).
- Types in the top-50 of 3 datasets (all VNC types, BANC + MCNS + MANC): an02a001, an05b009, an05b097, an09b009, an09b023, an19b001, anxxx027, in07b002, in10b011, in13b010. Brain types in the top-50 of both FAFB and BANC include CB1231 (FAFB rank 1; Δinfluence 2.69%), VESa1_P02, CB0251, CB0587, OA-VUMa4 (FAFB/MCNS), lLN2T_b, AL-AST1, M_l2PN3t18, M_l2PNl20, mZ_lv2PN, il3LN6 and vLN25. Several are antennal-lobe local or projection neuron types (lLN2T_b, il3LN6, vLN25, M_l2PN3t18, M_l2PNl20, mZ_lv2PN, V_ilPN), so in brain datasets many of the most central single types sit early in the olfactory pathway.

Holm correction across the six primary tests is not applied here, because the structural track produces bootstrap CIs, not p-values, for H1/H2. The metabolic and hub-targeted ratio CIs exclude 1 by a wide margin (largest upper bound 0.672, MANC metabolic_syn). Focal-lesion CIs reach 0.91–1.01 and are not treated as support.

## 5. Interpretation for the brain-death / resuscitation programme

1. Under random structural loss, fly sensorimotor reachability degrades gradually and has no sharp percolation threshold below f ≈ 0.7. A structural "point of no return" under random cell death is therefore not supported in these graphs.
2. Loss that preferentially kills large, high-synapse-count (high-demand) neurons, as a metabolic insult is hypothesised to do, halves reach at roughly a third of the damage that random loss needs (FAFB D50 0.288 area-weighted vs 0.759 random). Hub loss halves it at f = 0.028–0.115 in the brain-containing datasets (MANC 0.384). So *which* neurons die matters more than *how many*. This argues for prioritising protection of hub and high-demand neurons in any resuscitation protocol tested in the simulator.
3. Under random loss, weighted routing and the recurrent core fail before feedforward sensorimotor reachability. A damaged-then-reperfused network could keep its sensorimotor routes but process signals differently, which is the structural basis for "resuscitated but different".
4. There is no single keystone cell type. Single-type loss costs ≤ ~1% of reach, so structural fragility is distributed across many hubs, not concentrated in a few named cells.
5. All of this is model-structural. It says nothing about whether biological brain death is reversible.

## 6. Deviations from the task and preregistration

- MAOL v1.1 was not available locally and was not analysed (planned deviation).
- Concurrent CPU and RAM contention from the simulator agent: per-process CPU fell to about 15% of a core, and one MCNS keystone run hit an out-of-memory error and was restarted. Removal orders were therefore reduced below the prereg's ≥20: FAFB random 13, metabolic_area 10, metabolic_syn 5, degree 3 (+2 fine-grid), focal 5, matched 3. BANC random 15, metabolic_syn 8, degree 3, focal 4, matched 2. MCNS random 6, metabolic_syn 6, degree 2, focal 3, matched 2. MANC was at full design (20/20/5/10/5). The f grid was reduced from 24 to 13 points mid-run (subset). The Linf cap went from 60 to 30 hops for later orders. GSCC was computed only for orders 0–2.
- Controls were reduced: one rewired null graph (FAFB, MANC only; 3+1 / 2+1 orders); ≥10 cut-off with 2–3 random + 1 hub order; ≥1 only for MANC (the FAFB/MCNS files are floored at 5). Reach was not recomputed on a FAFB ≥1 graph from the no-threshold file.
- Keystone candidates were capped at the top-150 types by flow centrality, plus the replication-pass extras (4 FAFB, 68 BANC, 44 MCNS, 11 MANC). The top-50 rank is over evaluated types only.
- The primary metric samples 128 source neurons (stratified) rather than all ~17k sensory neurons.
- A CSV column-misalignment bug affected rows written without GSCC (f landed in the gscc column). 143 FAFB, 78 BANC and 65 MCNS rows were repaired deterministically; every misplaced value lay on the f grid, confirming the shift.
- The bootstrap used 2000 resamples for the primary metric on the ≥5 graph and 100 for controls and secondary metrics.
